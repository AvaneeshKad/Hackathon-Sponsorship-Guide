# Hackathon-Sponsorship-Guide
This repo has all you need to get your hackathon sponsorship

## Start

The first thing you do is **Plan**.

Set up the Google Drive/Onedrive/Cloud Storage/Self-hosted Folder that contains :
- Sponsor Tracking Sheet
- Previous Contacts
- Team Member info
- Meeting Minutes
- Sponsorship Deck

Management is the key to a successful organizing team.

#### *Speaking of management : Lets talk about **Time Management***

There is only one thing to remember : Do it ASAP!

But here's one caveat - Dont rush without preparation. Otherwise, you would get nothing but disappointment.

## Preparation

### Discussing with the team
  - What's the goal?
  - Who are we?
  - What does our hackathon/event offer?
  - How can we add more value and incentives for the corporate-companies/startups/sponsors?
  - Timeframe of the event
  - Venue
  - Sponsors Online or Offline
  - How is the Judging process going to be?
  - The Sponsorship Tiers
  - Flexibility in the tiers

### Sponsorship Deck

Making the sponsorship deck is not a light job. 
It takes many iterations, changes, and removal of extra stuff.

Having a sponsorship deck at the start helps build an image of the hackathon that is crucial for the company to remember your org as a unique identity.

What the deck should include :

 **Primary** :
 
  - Introduction to your Hackathon - with a punch line that is a selling point for your event.
  - Sponsorship Tier Table - Everything should be visible properly(Do not make it cluttered!)
  - Flexibilty options/Separate Tracks/Special Funds Option
  - Contact info
  - Past Hackathon photos

  **Add-ons** : 

  - Featured Projects
  - Different Ideas
  - Custom Hackathon Tracks 
  - Past Data / Statistics (if any)
  - Recommendation/ Positive Comments from faculy/professor/student-orgs/judges/mentors/past-sponsors
  - Collage of past sponsors

The sponsorship deck should have a nice theme/graphics.
Can add a mascot (if possible)

**The Important part is having a sponsorship deck that is enthusiastic but professional at the same time.**

## Sponsorship Tiers

Divide the things you offer in 3 parts 

- Talent Acquisition
- Engagement
- Brand Presence

#### Talent Acquisition

This is most common thing which most of the corporate companies want. **"*Hiring Talent*"**

These are the points which your hackathon can offer :
- Resumes
- Github
- LinkedIn
- Email Blast to everyone regarding the open positions in the company
- Sponsor Booth : Where the hackers can interact with the recruiter and drop-in resumes or talk about projects
- Custom Hackathon Track for a particular problem-statement that company is interested in.

#### Engagement

Some people really value building a tech-community!

There are the points that can engage the companies with the hackers : 

- Workshops
- Mentors
- Judges
- Giving Opening Ceremony Speech
- Dedicated Chat Room
- Distributing API keys or tools

#### Brand Presence

Usually small companies/startups want to gain their name in the local tech-community. It helps them grow, get more investers, and visibility.

Brand presence can be made by : 

- Social Media Posts (Instagram, tiktok, Linkedin)
- Website
- Brand logo on t-shirts (Smaller name for small sponsors, bigger name for bigger sponsors)
- Sponsor's Swag Giveaway
- Bigger Sponsor Booth based on sponsor tier (Silver, Gold, etc)


Based on all of this - there should be 3-4 tiers.

For eg :

-> White($500), Bronze($1,000), Silver($3,000), Gold($5,000)

This helps **managing** sponsors.

## Gathering Contacts and Research

Now comes the struggling phase.

This is where your **Networking Skills** would shin.

In your contacts excel sheet add all the recruiter/job interviewer/HRs/employee/past employee/ceo/founders' email or contact number. Include their company name as well to keep a fairly organized record.

**Identifying potential sponsors**

Google search, indeed, other hackathon's sponsors, and more are some of the sources from where you can find the target companies.

- The companies/startup/potential sponsor should be have some of their work in tech, software, R&D, robotics-hardware, electronics, and web development.
- The company should be located in your country.
- The company that is actively hiring.
- The company having no criminal or non-ethical records.

Go through linkedin and search for University Recruiter or HR of the target company.

Try to msg on Linkedin but thats very ineffective so always try to find their contact number or company email id.

This is where your Stalking, OSINT, and Reconnaissance skills come into play.

It takes a lot of time on internet to find the contacts and it is completely fine if you can't search for it. **Do not tamper with anyone's Privacy.**

## Cold Emailing

By far the easiest step.

Do not underestimate it, one should be consistent weekly for emailing potential sponsors.

Here - **Following up is the Key.**

***If you dont follow up then dont expect any reply from them. Period.***

- Schedule your emails. 
  - Anywhere from 8am to 5pm
  - Do not spam anyone, it is not nice.
  - One email to one person at a time.
  - Try not to send emails on weekend or national holidays.
- Do not send only one email to multiple addresses or CC or BCC
- Have a template ready
- Be Kind and Polite
- Always Customize your email to the target companies' needs or background.
- Ask them if they have any problem statement to solve.
- Attach the Sponsorship Deck pdf in your first email.
- Ask for a short call/ small chat/Grab a coffee etc
- Always Check for Spelling mistakes. 
- Mention your contact details in your signature.
- Try to keep the email in medium size - not too short not too big. If it get big, you can provide a TL;DR section.
- Remember, they are human too. Mistakes can happen from their end. Be Kind!

Don't be surprised if you dont receive any reply to your email at all even if you followed up 100 times.

After each email, update your **Tracking Sheet** with the latest interaction date and some notes (Eg:- "Followed up but automated reply says the person is out of town.")
